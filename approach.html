Business first. Technology second.

Good solutions start with a clear understanding of how a business actually works. This is the process I follow, with examples from my own work.

1

Understand

Before anything is designed, I make sure the real question is clear: what the business is trying to achieve, who is affected, and which constraints are fixed.

What it produces

  • Stakeholder conversations
  • Problem statement
  • Scope and constraints

In practice

On the AI deployment sprint, separating what must stay (existing production systems) from what can change shaped every option that followed.

2

Map

I document how work actually happens, step by step, including the workarounds people rely on but rarely write down.

What it produces

  • Current-state process maps
  • BPMN models
  • Data flow diagrams

In practice

At Brand Addition, I wrote the department's first official workflow document, turning tacit knowledge into a standard everyone could follow.

3

Diagnose

I look for the root cause rather than the most visible symptom, and I check that the data reflects reality before relying on it.

What it produces

  • Root cause analysis
  • Data validation
  • Reconciliations

In practice

Reconciling inventory and validating courier data imports at Brand Addition reduced dispatch errors at their source.

4

Appraise

I compare realistic options side by side, including buying, building and simple improvements, so the decision rests on evidence about cost, risk and value.

What it produces

  • Options appraisal
  • Decision matrix
  • Build or buy comparison

In practice

On the sprint, the team is weighing build and buy routes rather than assuming that new software is the answer.

5

Design

I define what the solution must do, how it connects to existing systems and how we will know it works, keeping people in control where judgement matters.

What it produces

  • Requirements
  • Integration and architecture design
  • Acceptance criteria

In practice

Nupcial enforces data isolation in the database itself, so security does not depend on the interface behaving correctly.

6

Deliver and measure

I deliver in small iterations and measure whether the change achieved its purpose, then improve from there.

What it produces

  • Iterative delivery
  • KPI dashboards
  • Post-launch review

In practice

Nupcial went from prototype to a live platform used by real guests, and Excel KPI reporting at Brand Addition tracked a 45% gain in resource allocation efficiency.

Principles I hold to

Evidence over assumption

Decisions rest on how the process really works and what the data really shows.

The simplest option that works

A focused fix that solves most of the problem can be worth more than a large system that solves all of it.

People stay in control

Automation and AI handle repetitive work; people keep the decisions that need judgement.

Data handled responsibly

Organisational data only goes into tools that the organisation has approved.
