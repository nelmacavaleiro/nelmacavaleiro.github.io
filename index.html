Nupcial: architecting and launching a two-sided SaaS platform Founder and developer, February 2026 to present Solution architectureFull-stack developmentPayments

Context

Nupcial is a wedding planning platform with two sides: couples who plan their wedding, and vendors who offer services to them. It is live at nupcial.app.

Approach

I produced a first prototype from a single prompt in Google AI Studio at a Manchester Digital event, then rebuilt the product entirely over approximately three months, using Claude Code for AI-assisted development and debugging. I owned the full lifecycle: product concept, UX and UI, architecture, development and deployment.

Key decisions

  • Row-level security in the database, so each couple can only ever read and change their own wedding data.
  • Idempotent wedding creation at sign-up, preventing duplicate records when a request is repeated.
  • Free for couples, with a commission on vendor transactions through Stripe Connect, to lower the barrier to adoption on the demand side.

Outcome

Publicly launched and in ongoing development. I used Nupcial to plan my own wedding, and our guests used it on the day.

Features delivered

Guest and seating management with drag and drop, budget tracking, vendor onboarding and pipeline, wedding album and live slideshow, and an AI assistant.

Tools

React, Vite, Zustand, Supabase, Stripe Connect, Git and GitHub, Claude Code

MSc research: the influence of Business Intelligence in project management University of Sheffield, 2026 ResearchBusiness IntelligenceMixed methods

Context

My MSc Information Systems Management dissertation examined how Business Intelligence is used in project work, and what stands between the two disciplines.

Method

A mixed-methods study combining 11 semi-structured interviews with project management and Business Intelligence practitioners and 33 survey responses, conducted with university ethics approval.

Themes explored

  • Familiarity with BI and the organisational context.
  • How BI tools are used in project work.
  • The effect on project performance and decision-making.
  • Challenges, barriers and the gap between BI and project management.
  • The experience of people who hold both responsibilities.

How I approach a problem

Business first, technology second. The right answer is sometimes a small change rather than a new system.

  1. Understand

    Clarify the business goal, who is affected and what cannot change.

  2. Map

    Document how the process works today, not how it is assumed to work.

  3. Diagnose

    Find root causes using evidence and data, not opinion.

  4. Appraise

    Compare options, including build, buy and simple improvements, against cost, risk and value.

  5. Design

    Define requirements, integrations and acceptance criteria, keeping people in control where judgement matters.

  6. Deliver and measure

    Deliver in iterations and check whether the change achieved its purpose.
